Today is the first day of the new year, and I want to share my plans and goals for it. I hope these plans will help me improve in the areas I said in my previous two reviews I did poorly, and that I can make progress and move one step closer to the person I want to become.

## (1) Short-Term Goals

In my 2019 review I mentioned that I am facing some difficulties at work, so in the first quarter of 2020, the most pressing task is to get out of my current predicament. This has two parts:

1. Achieve more in my current job.

Objectively speaking, even though my situation is unfavorable, my current work still has a certain importance. On that basis, I can treat the present pressure as a chance to raise my level at work, and even my general level of getting things done. The “unfair” treatment I sometimes receive at work is also often a chance to be trained beyond my grade. Some simple goals:

- Set some OKRs beyond what is required for the projects of the first half of the year and try to complete them, with weekly and monthly summaries and assessments
- Record and archive all the knowledge and skills in my work
- Proactively reach out to business stakeholders, proactively gauge the business value of projects and how far-sighted the technology is, and periodically summarize a SWOT analysis of the current work and team and the short- and long-term market trends. Summarize the patterns of technological evolution in this industry.
- Proactively talk with my boss and offer my opinions and suggestions. Summarize the experience and lessons of team management at work.
- Summarize the differences in how people of high and low levels do things, based on what I learn from both.

2. Consider changing jobs.

The reasons for changing jobs are not a temporary difficulty; they are more about many personal factors that are hard to resolve through effort. The most important reasons come down to two points:

- **Be with people who are better than you.** This is a rule I endorse and follow. I am good at learning from others and seeing their strengths, while also looking objectively at my level and theirs. My current colleagues and boss are all very smart, and I have learned quite a few ways of doing things from them that are better than mine. But overall, from an engineer’s point of view, their non-technical side is lacking, and in the environment of a non-technology company, this imbalance’s limit on long-term development, for individuals and teams alike, is especially obvious. (Being able to see this clearly so early in my career, in the finance industry, is a kind of luck.) From this angle, I hope to find people who are better than me in management, business and product, and learn and improve from them.
- **The system is greater than the individual.** This is a view from *Whiplash*, one of the books that influenced me most in 2019. I believe it is especially important in the industry I work in. Within three to five years, being able to build an efficient, healthy system, team and methodology is the foundation of winning. In my two years at work, my own team has been going through considerable change. My own view is that the team’s business model is very successful and has a good chance of transforming from a small startup-like team into a mature large team. But in this transition I am in a position within the team that bears relatively more risk for lower returns. Being far from the center of power and handling cross-cutting business are risks I appreciate more after starting work.

In concrete terms, I hope to follow these principles:

1. **Make choices that fit long-term development.** Predicaments and “pressing needs” that must be solved are things one often has to face in life, but even when time and choices are limited, one should try to think things through. Avoid “when the poor lack ambition,” making choices under pressure.
2. **Short-term goals should serve long-term goals.** I hope the job search can also serve to summarize my knowledge system and accumulate industry experience, so that, whether it succeeds or not, I make progress.
3. **Summarize repeatable successful experience.** Set and complete some OKRs. Seize every opportunity and mobilize the resources around me as much as possible. And record the experience gained along the way. “How many times in life can one go all out?” Facing the challenge, let me spend a fulfilling stretch of time without regret.

## (2) Long-Term Goals

This does not mean things I must keep at for a very long time, but things I plan to do this year that cultivate my character and habits and benefit long-term development.

1. **Keep writing**

Starting from the three pieces I wrote at the end of 2019, complete 30 to 50 articles. The content should progress gradually from personal reflections, study records and reflections to systematic study summaries and interpretations of some questions. Along the way, accumulate excellent sources of material and information to enrich my learning resources. If time permits, read a few books on expression and writing, take one or two writing courses, and summarize some techniques of expression and writing.

I do not myself like writing, and I even used to admire “transmitting without creating.” The reason I am spending time training my writing is that, to me, writing is a skill that can increase my scarcity, and also one that can concretely help my present work and life. For me, the two abilities I most need to improve through writing are these:

- **The ability to think deeply.** In life and at work, my impression of myself is that my thinking leans toward being a “quick thinker.” This has to do with the broad range of knowledge I was exposed to from childhood and with my conversations with my parents and friends. In study and work I am good at proposing multiple solutions in a short time, gathering all kinds of information, thinking by forward transfer, and giving quick feedback in expression. By contrast, I am lacking in deep thinking. And in an era in which people “look at their phones every six minutes on average,” deep thinking and deep work are scarcer skills. The ability to shut out the distraction of information and think deeply about a problem is what I hope to train. Writing is a tool that helps unfold chains of logic and slows people down and shuts out distraction. Forming a systematic writing habit is an entry-level method for deep thinking.
- **The ability to communicate and tell stories.** Objectively, this ability is scarcer among Chinese than among Westerners, and scarcer among engineers than among liberal-arts and business professionals. In a small way, for many engineers this is a short leg limiting their ability with business products. In a larger way, in the present age, how effectively one conveys information and how far one expands one’s influence determines how successful one can be.

**2. Learn systematically and organize my knowledge system.**

In the 2019 review I stressed the importance of systematic learning. This year I hope to improve my learning methods and tools through practice by getting several things done.

- Summarize my knowledge system and what I have already learned. Overall, within my current circle I am the type who learns a rather miscellaneous range of knowledge, with varying degrees of exposure from undergraduate business management, economics and finance to mathematics, programming and statistical learning. I think I will take about six to eight months (also the time I will be preparing for the job search) to summarize my knowledge system, organize it into a T-shaped structure, and choose a direction to go deep in. At the same time, for each branch, I will organize the information sources that influenced me most (for example, online sources, books, people), the people at a high level in the field and trends worth following, and methods of learning and distinctive viewpoints. In my own specialty, I will identify the important problems currently faced and the main thread of learning.
- Learn some new things. For this, first finish some online courses I did not finish last year, and also hope to turn the books I have read into organized material. This year there are also a few books I want to read and online courses I want to take (information theory, game theory and economic history), and I hope to complete them in the second half of the year if time permits.

**3. Summarize my understanding of economics and investing, and put it into practice.**

I am a professional in the financial investment industry, and from the standpoint of both career development and personal life it is necessary to summarize the simple rules and practices in this area, especially now that there is so much mixed-quality information online. This can also help those around me establish the concept of long-term investing, and help me build habits of investing and financial planning.

**4. Build some good habits in life.**

As I said in the 2019 review, this may be the most important aspect. Besides writing regularly and keeping up the good habits of 2019, the habits I hope to build in 2020 are:

- Orderliness: do fixed things at fixed times. This way I do fewer unnecessary things, and it is also easier to stick with one thing.
- Focus: do one thing at a time and keep up the time I spend on the same thing. Use the Pomodoro technique and reduce time spent looking at my phone.
- Regularly talk with friends, sharing my thoughts and learning from them.

## (3) A Few Words for Myself

I am someone who likes making plans but often fails to finish them, because although I understand the principle of “aim high, and you will get the middle,” I am still wary that plans will increase my perfectionist tendencies and reduce my room to maneuver when faced with change. But I think that, written down or not, everyone has plans and goals in their heart and a self they want to become. We live in an age full of change, opportunity and uncertainty; whether or not this year’s goals are completed, and however many new changes and challenges this year brings, doing my best means no regrets. Here I excerpt three sentences I like, and hope to keep in mind when I meet difficulties this year.

1. **Anyone, whatever their starting point, as long as they recognize their position, find the right direction, do things in the right way, raise their speed of progress and keep a good rhythm, will in a few years see a self with a far bigger horizon than today, a self who feels that life was not lived in vain.** This is a line from Mr. Wu Jun. I think that along the way, although I took many detours and made many mistakes, I tried hard at every opportunity in life, and gained many extraordinary and unforgettable experiences and much knowledge. I hope to keep it up, and in a few years still feel that my life was not lived in vain.

2. **Man’s dearest possession is life. It is given to him but once, and he must live it so as to feel no torturing regrets for wasted years, never know the burning shame of a mean and petty past.** I think that whatever my present level, environment and luck, as long as tomorrow’s me is better than today’s, I have not wasted my years or lived without achievement. And the standard of “better” is in each person’s own heart.

3. **With a good conscience our only sure reward.**

I also wish everyone progress in the new year!
