Five Agents, One Coach
Why I'm still building a growth system for myself in the age of AI

A general Personal Agent is an ever more capable assistant; what I'm building is a coach. It isn't measured by how much AI does for me, but by whether I get stronger. The two differ not in technology but in their objective function.

Conclusions first: four judgments, five-part argument

It starts with a very personal question (why does effort stop being enough after thirty?) and ends with a narrow decision.

  • ProductsInformation Assistant, Agentic Knowledge Bank, Learning OS and Writing Assistant form a "cognitive machine" for building real capability over time; Compass is a personal strategy system for deciding where in the real world that capability should go.
  • ThreadCognition is extracting invariants from phenomena; it needs Connect (breadth) and Think (depth); what actually changes it is feedback. After school an adult's feedback system takes four cuts; each cognitive product repairs one.
  • PositionHuman-centered from the start. Muse, Dots and Grok Bot made memory, proactivity and multi-agent work standard, which only sharpens the line: they are service agents that amplify a person's convenience, not their ability; I'm building a coach, a design that is the opposite of a Personal Agent.
  • DecisionBuy every general capability; invest only in feedback and evaluation, cognitive challenge, judgment history and personal strategy. Prove on myself that a person really grows before talking about a product.
The test · prove it on myself first

The control group is a well-configured general Personal Agent. If in twelve weeks this system doesn't make me measurably better at independent learning, long-term judgment or real-world decisions, I accept that the general platform is good enough.

How cognition grows · invariants, breadth and depth, feedback

Cognition aims at invariants; reaching them takes two dimensions, Connect and Think; and what actually changes cognition is the error signal. This part is the foundation of the whole system.

  • Phenomena → invariants
  • Connect × Think · T-shaped

Cognition is extracting invariants from phenomena

Cognitive growth isn't knowing more phenomena; it's extracting structural patterns that explain and predict other phenomena. An invariant isn't forever true: it keeps explaining across settings, and new evidence must be able to revise it.

Example 1 · the AI industry

Phenomena: a model ships a new version, a company raises a round, an agent adds a feature, a benchmark falls.

The real cognitive questions: which part of the cost structure is technology changing? What is going from scarce to commodity? Where in the value chain is value moving? Which business models become possible, and which advantages erode?

Example 2 · interviews and partnerships

Phenomena: concrete feedback from one interview or one partnership.

Structural judgment: "The market recognizes my technical depth, but my business articulation hasn't earned the same value." That can change my learning focus, career choices, even how I position myself.

The line

The first is information. Only the second starts to be cognition.

Traditional notes are good at storing phenomena, rarely help me find invariants, and rarely tell me when today's evidence conflicts with an old judgment. Ten thousand notes don't mean you understand the world.

2 · How cognition grows: from phenomena to invariants (appearances vs. stable structures and laws)

Horizontal Connect, vertical Think, and a cave to protect depth

Breadth of knowledge can grow fast through exposure. Depth of cognition only builds slowly, through intense independent thinking and feedback.

  • Connect · breadthRoom to compare and transfer, so frameworks collide: an algorithms-only background treats business problems as optimization; a business-only one underrates how technology shifts marginal cost. The risk: it slides easily into information consumption.
  • Think · depthKeep drilling into one question. With agents you can stop at frameworks and papers, or ask: how do they really differ from traditional software? Does reliability come from the model or the system design? Which classic computing ideas does it echo?
  • T-shapedThey complement each other and can't substitute. Depth without breadth becomes a local optimum; breadth without depth is just upscale information consumption.
The cave · protecting depth in an over-connected world

Not physical isolation but a cognitively independent space: outside information is filtered, not cut off; you think about organized material, not an endless feed; what matters isn't what comes next but whether you've truly thought this one problem through.

Most knowledge tools optimize only Connect. I want to engineer the other dimension: build myself a cave that cuts noise and takes in the questions worth thinking about, then carry the judgment back out to face new evidence.

AI sharpens it

AI makes breadth nearly free and skipping the vertical work easier: once a problem gets hard, we ask AI. An answer is far from mastery. Understanding the answer ≠ rebuilding the reasoning on your own.

Alternate

Connecting goes out into the world; thinking returns to the cave. Connect without thinking and you lose your judgment; think without connecting and you drift from reality.

2 · How cognition grows: horizontal Connect, vertical Think, and the cave

The five-level learning ladder: five ever more expensive kinds of feedback

Cognition is set by error signals, not inputs. Only experience corrected by an error signal becomes real experience. Otherwise ten years at work may be the first year repeated ten times.

Lived ≠ learned

Ten years of blaming failure on the environment, colleagues and luck, never revising the framework, may be the first year repeated ten times.

Shared variable

How deeply you expose yourself to the risk of being proven wrong, and how many people are entitled to say so.

Self-protection

The upper levels keep making you face "I hadn't actually thought this through." The barrier to learning isn't only intelligence; it's self-protection.

2 · How cognition grows: from listening to writing

After school, the feedback system takes four cuts

Exams don't measure every ability, but they keep telling us what's right and wrong. After graduation, that machinery gets dismantled piece by piece.

  • 01 Cut-offThe tests are gone; no one tells you whether you're right. Reading, courses, bookmarks and summaries feel like progress but prove no real mastery.
  • 02 ContaminationWe turn to consuming information, and two filters push the same way: you grow more certain and narrower. The filter bubble is built jointly by platforms and by us.
  • 03 Wrong targetOnly single-loop review. Double-loop asks "could this goal itself be wrong?", which threatens identity, so we avoid it. Optimizing only execution makes you ever better at things not worth doing.
  • 04 No traceReviews rely on memory; notes sit in a dozen apps. Without judgment history you can't see recurring bias, and every time you start from zero.
Veteran mode

Cut off from outside correction, receiving only confirmation, ever more skilled at the wrong goal, leaving no trace. The ego becomes the last immune wall: any painful feedback can be explained away as "they don't get it."

AI's opening

AI's greatest value may not be reading and summarizing for us, but that for the first time it could reinstall a feedback system for an adult.

3 · Five products: repairing four breaks in the feedback loop

Information Assistant and Knowledge Bank: a radar, and an auditable cognitive model

Information Assistant · repairs "contamination"

  • ScoringAgainst my own cognitive framework, not a generic "quality content" bar; sources graded by evidence, with trust as its own axis, not a proxy for popularity
  • Counter-evidenceWhen I'm bullish on an AI direction, it actively looks for failed cases, rival paths and objections; value neutrality is a product requirement, not an attitude
  • SignalSeparates new facts, repeat coverage, opinion in packaging, and signals that could actually change a judgment
  • Shape · rhythmFlags inputs that are too concentrated, blind spots and cross-field links; decides when to keep exploring and when to stop and go deep
  • AlertsOnly when an important judgment is challenged, never a daily digest
The failure

A product that only feeds me what fits my framework ends up no different from a short-video platform. Its value is pointing out why this article might change the judgment I made three months ago.

Agentic Knowledge Bank · repairs "no trace"

  • SourcingEvery paragraph traces to a source; facts, claims and inferences kept apart, so every key conclusion can be traced
  • Explicit gapsWhere no material supports it, it must say "this is empty" and fill nothing in; the most counterintuitive requirement
  • StructureOrganizes scattered knowledge into concepts, causal links and conditions of use; buys document import, vector search and other basics
  • Conflict · returnFinds contradictions across sources and over time; sends gaps back to Information Assistant and Learning OS as new research questions
  • BoundaryThe knowledge bank manages the knowledge model; cross-product memory manages judgment and feedback history. They overlap, but they're not the same thing
Principle

An honest gap is more useful than a beautiful story. A gap tells you what to read next; a story only tells you that you already understand. It's the most concrete form of the cave.

3 · Five products: not competing with general-agent memory, and not a more complicated Notion

Learning OS & Writing Assistant: reinstall exams, challenge thinking

Learning OS · repairs "cut-off"

Finishing a book doesn't count, nor does finishing a course or getting AI to write beautiful notes. Mastery means calling on it independently, with no hints, on an unfamiliar problem.

  • Cold recallRetrieval attempts with no hints at all
  • Spacing · resetCapability decays; reassess after a delayed retest
  • Help levelRecorded apart from correctness: getting it right and how many hints it took are two things
  • Evidence levelsCan recall, apply, explain, generate; no fake numeric scores
  • Sealed bankQuestions sealed before learning test the system itself; otherwise it only trains "the ability to pass this system"
  • Map · transferMastery standards by domain, skill and difficulty; transfer tested with unpracticed problem types or real projects

Ladder → five kinds of capability evidence, judged per domain

  1. 1Explain
  2. 2Derive alone
  3. 3Solve new problems
  4. 4Actually build
  5. 5Produce systematic work
Bottom line

AI can prepare material, design exercises and find mistakes, but the thinking has to be mine. Otherwise it's the AI getting smarter, not me.

Writing Assistant · repairs "wrong target"

An idea kept in your head feels complete. Step one isn't polish but interrogation:

What's the core claim?What's the evidence?Any other explanation?Why does it support the conclusion?What's new here?
  1. Force a thesis: press loose ideas into one arguable core claim
  2. Opposing review: from other theories, other stakeholders, and the facts
  3. Separate sources: my own judgment / citations / AI additions
  4. Expression last: drafting, polish, translation, layout and publishing can be bought
  5. Write back: pull evidence-based criticism from outside feedback into my views and knowledge model
Why writing

The only act that makes the framework itself an object: written, its cracks show; published, strangers test it. It takes cognitive feedback out of my sole control.

Division of labor

Learning OS tests predefined knowledge and capability; Writing Assistant tests the frameworks and original judgments I propose. A polished AI-generated view must never pass as my own cognitive work.

A loop, not a pipeline: four cognitive products make one machine

If the four products each work alone, they're still just four tools. What matters is the loop between them.

  • IA → AKBOutside information helps me find the problem
  • AKB → LOSThe knowledge bank places it in my existing framework
  • LOS → WAThe learning system checks whether I've really mastered it
  • WA → worldWriting puts the view up for outside testing
  • WA ⇢ IALogical flaws exposed in writing become new research questions
  • LOS ⇢ AKBCapability gaps exposed in practice are written back as gaps in the knowledge bank
  • AKB ⇢ IAUnexplained gaps decide what information to seek next round
The machine

A pipeline runs one way; a loop has return paths. This cognitive machine keeps doing three things: meet the world, build a model, take correction.

Today

The return edges aren't connected yet: writing's questions don't flow back to information gathering, and practice blind spots aren't written back to the knowledge bank. That's the first next step (see 05).

3 · Five products: without the adversarial reading, it's just a smarter action planner

Two loops, joined by judgment history

The first four products form a loop of cognition and capability building; Compass forms a loop of running myself against market feedback. Kept apart, the two loops can't compound.

  • Capability → reality: if a skill built in Learning OS or a judgment formed in writing never reaches Compass, strategy can't see I've changed or spot new openings.
  • Reality → capability: the market rates my technical skills highly but demands more of some business skill; that feedback should change what I learn next.
  • Further: my strongest technical skill may not be what customers most want to pay for, while a skill I've underrated is what makes the opportunity work. That should change my learning focus and framework, even make me drop the original direction.
Both ways

Capability building must face the market, and market feedback must drive capability building; otherwise learning turns into self-satisfied hoarding and strategy into a busy opportunity-chasing machine.

The other risk

Short-term rewards from the real world mustn't dominate cognitive building either, or I'm back to chasing hot topics, a local optimum. Long-term hypotheses and short-term evidence both have to stay.

Over time it should answer: which judgment biases recurred this year? Which capabilities did the market keep validating? Which long-held bets never produced value? Which lucky breaks actually came from years of accumulation?

Compounding

Only when history can be compared, judgments tracked and mistakes reviewed does "personal compounding" stop being a metaphor.

Two things I won't hand to AI: judgment and the opponent

The built-in tension: if AI handles information, knowledge, learning support and strategy analysis well, do I lose exactly the abilities I meant to build? Like letting a machine lift the weights at the gym and logging the reps as your own.

The first · judgment

What has taste, what's useful, what's worth investing in, what isn't worth doing

  • AI can assist but can't carry it for me. Not because AI must be worse; in more and more areas it may judge better than I do
  • If I just accept its conclusions every time, it's the AI's model that grows, not mine; I get the answer but not the capability
  • Every important choice leaves my reasons, uncertainty and evidence; AI may question and correct, but whether to revise is my call
Data design

Keeping the human in the loop isn't an interaction-design decision but a data-design one: my judgment history is my framework. Without that history the framework can be neither injected nor iterated.

The second · the opponent

You can't see your own position clearly from inside it

  • Experience breeds self-consistency, success makes flaws harder to accept, and with authority fewer people correct you
  • A system that only learns my preferences, views and habits will know me better and flatter me better: the most dangerous failure
  • It must stand outside my framework and ask: your evidence? Another explanation? Made this misjudgment before? If the premise is wrong, what will it cost?
  • Real challenge also comes from the world: paying customers, actual use, peer critique, unrehearsed problems
Scarcity

When every free product makes money by pleasing its users, not pleasing them is itself scarce. If this ever charges money, what it should sell is this: it doesn't need to please me.

The standard

A system that truly serves cognitive growth can't just make me feel understood; it has to keep me exposed to evidence that might prove me wrong.

4 · A coach, not an assistant: search, parsing, generation, calendar and execution were always external

Two objective functions: Agent Productivity vs. Human Capability Development

Cognitive labor is ever easier to outsource. What used to take me three hours of research, framing and reasoning, an agent can now hand back as a report.

  • General AgentMake AI know you ever better and do more of your work. More automation, less time spent, preferences met more precisely.
  • CoachHelp you understand the world and yourself. Deeper understanding, more transferable skills, judgment that holds up to testing.
  • Same for CompassHaving it finish a career plan for me doesn't prove my strategic judgment improved.
A subtler tension · preference

The better an agent knows my style, preferences and habits, the more easily it does tasks the way I always have, while cognitive growth sometimes demands breaking exactly those habits.

I've long been excited by a certain kind of technical opportunity. A preference-optimizing system gets ever better at finding me more of them; the system I actually need checks whether I'm repeating a long-running judgment bias.

Not contrarianism

Objection without evidence is just noise. What has value is challenge grounded in my history and real outcomes, which can itself be tested.

4 · A coach, not an assistant: the difference between Agent Productivity and Human Capability Development

Waiter and coach: the same technology, different objective functions

A system built around consuming content and a system built around cognitive growth will make completely different product decisions, even on the exact same model.

A truly effective coach may need to do the opposite

  • Wants an answerHold the answer back; ask them to derive it first
  • Loves a directionDon't serve more of the same; show the counter-evidence
  • Wants ten fieldsAsk them to master one problem first
  • Chasing ten leadsSuggest dropping eight of them
  • Wants praiseTell them: you made no real progress in the last three months
The difference

A waiter works to meet the needs you've stated; a good coach is obliged to question whether those needs actually serve your goals.

The dial

The coaching-intensity dial is always turned by the human; the agent can't turn it up on its own. Not every interaction should challenge; the right feedback at the right moment, or coaching turns into preaching.

Tension

Mass-market products optimize first for value deliverable at scale; deep cognitive growth is slow, effortful, personal and unpleasant in the short run. And a coach can't take away autonomy "for your own good."

4 · A coach, not an assistant: commercial doesn't mean shallow; Dedao charges too, and so do coaches

Connect · Think · Test · Act, and redefining success

The five products stop being five separate categories; they become five sets of skills one coach uses for different growth goals.

  • Info AssistantA research assistant that builds breadth and keeps me open
  • Knowledge BankLets my cognitive model be externalized, audited and iterated
  • Learning OSA coach for deliberate practice and measuring capability
  • Writing AssistantA mentor that tests understanding through writing and fights fuzzy thinking
  • CompassA personal strategy coach connecting cognition and capability to real opportunities

Three success criteria · avoid building an AI coach that only looks strict

  1. Growth in human capability, not agent task volume: can I solve new problems without AI? Do I still have it three months later?
  2. Better judgment quality, not more "insights": how many key predictions and choices did later evidence support? Separate luck from the quality of the process.
  3. Real-world leverage, not activity inside the system: more valuable partnerships, better choices of opportunity, less long-run wasted effort, work and career assets that accumulate.
The mission

A coach's mission isn't to make users need a coach forever, but to keep raising their ability to do without one. Even as I use it less and less, my ability to think, judge and create value on my own should keep rising.

5 · How to build it: boundaries

Four cross-product capabilities, unified in the Judgment Record

The Judgment Record isn't a database design but a principle every product follows: a judgment has a complete life cycle.

  • Cognitive modelNot just remembering interests and preferences, but understanding my frameworks, long-term hypotheses, knowledge gaps, real capability and judgment biases, evolving with learning and real feedback
  • Feedback & evaluationThe one most worth deep research. How do you design effective feedback in different settings? How do you tell familiarity from independent mastery, sound judgment from a lucky outcome, real progress from AI doing the work?
  • Cognitive challengeNot perpetual contrarianism, but finding blind spots and counterexamples from evidence; knowing when to encourage exploration, when to demand independent thinking, when to question the goal itself
  • CompoundingKeep the grounds, actions, predictions and later outcomes of judgments over time, so the five products influence each other and connect cognitive assets, capability and real opportunities

If configuring Dots with five sets of long-running tasks, evaluation rubrics and feedback flows gets me most of the way, there's no need to rebuild five complete products. Five apps, five agents, five names are only possible forms.

Control group

The first comparison isn't Notion, Todoist or a traditional CRM, but a well-configured general Personal Agent. If in twelve weeks this system doesn't make me better at independent learning, long-term judgment or real decisions, I accept that the general platform is good enough.

5 · How to build it: separating "worth building for myself" from "worth building a company on"

My decisions, and where things stand

"Five Personal Agents" is the form; the real positioning is a Personal Intelligence Coach. That's a claim needing long-term validation, not a moat that already exists.

Four clear choices

  1. Five specialist boundaries, one unified coach: no five separate sets of goals, memory and feedback
  2. Buy general capabilities first: search, inference, parsing, generation and basic execution aren't the R&D battlefield
  3. Concentrate on feedback science, cognitive models and personal strategy: independent assessment, evidence-based challenge, long-term decision calibration
  4. Prove human growth first: not user numbers or agent strength, but a few real growth cases against a well-configured general agent
Personal Intelligence Coach

A personal system that uses AI to keep deepening cognition, calibrating judgment and building long-term compounding.

Unresolved

The learning system insists judgment be mine; the strategy system wants AI's help to judge opportunities well. Which judgments can go to an agent? How do I show it's augmenting my judgment, not replacing it?

Implemented · mostly methodology and contracts so far

  • Learning OSSealed question bank and full self-verification; the best-designed part
  • Knowledge BankPer-paragraph sourcing and explicit gaps
  • CompassA minimal "log, then review" rhythm
  • UnverifiedRuntime, end-to-end behavior, real data connections

Three systemic gaps · right where the core investment is

The challenge layer is almost zero

Every product has a natural place for it; none actually implements it. Without it, the whole system is just "better knowledge management."

Judgment memory is almost zero

Nowhere records "what I judged about what, and whether it proved right."

The pipeline isn't a loop yet

Return edges aren't connected; the cognitive machine and Compass share no capability claims, so what my profile claims and what Learning OS can evidence may never agree.

Next: three very small things

  1. Define the minimal Judgment Record: context, grounds, uncertainty at the time, final outcome
  2. Connect one return edge, even if it's just writing "what I couldn't explain" in practice back as one gap in the knowledge bank
  3. Run every product end to end once, for real

Six months to a year, tested in real life

  • Better-grounded long-term judgment, not more news
  • Deeper professional capability, not more courses
  • Lasting reciprocal relationships, not a longer contact list
  • Better allocation of resources, not more tasks done
  • Dropping wrong directions in time
Bottom line

Getting the loop to turn matters more than discussing the full architecture. If it doesn't improve real choices, however advanced it looks, it has failed.
